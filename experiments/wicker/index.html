In Wicker (2026), Wētā Workshop made Alexander Skarsgård’s wicker husband from 3D-printed appliances finished as cane, a weave that follows the planes of his face, flax for his hair and beard, and a little light through the gaps to show he’s hollow.

This does the same to a photo, on your device. It finds 478 points on each face and lays over them a weave drawn once on a standard face: rings round the eyes and mouth, a herringbone down the nose, arcs across the cheeks and a bound seam where panels meet. The weave is lit by the light your photo was taken in, worked out from the face itself. Hair turns to straw, combed the way yours lies, and bare skin gets a wider herringbone.

Keys: hold C to compare, S to save, space to take the portrait from the camera.
