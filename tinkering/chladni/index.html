In 1787 Ernst Chladni bowed the edge of a sand-covered brass plate and watched the sand gather into lines. The plate was ringing in one of its natural shapes. Where it moved, the sand was thrown off; where it held still, the sand stayed.

This plate is driven from the centre by a sine wave. Most frequencies only make it buzz. Near one of its resonances it rings, and the sand draws that resonance's still lines. Higher notes draw finer figures.

Try other materials. Liquids show the same wave in their surface, slowed down so you can see it; the flame table burns tallest where the plate moves most.
