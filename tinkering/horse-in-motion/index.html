In 1878 Leland Stanford wanted to settle an argument: does a galloping horse ever have all four hooves off the ground at once? It moves too fast for the eye to tell. Eadweard Muybridge set twelve cameras in a row beside the Palo Alto track, each fired by a thread the horse broke as it ran past.

The answer is in frames 2 and 3. The hooves do leave the ground, but with the legs tucked up under the belly, not stretched out fore and aft as painters had shown it for centuries.

These are the eleven gallop frames from the public-domain scan of the card on Wikimedia Commons, cropped and lined up on the rider and the track lines. The frames were about a twenty-fifth of a second apart, so real speed is 25 frames a second.
