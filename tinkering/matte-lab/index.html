Matte

A design system drawn from 80s and 90s matte black electronics: powder-coated bodies, machined seams, printed legends and one warm accent. Everything here is built from matte.css. The bar above tunes the tokens, and every specimen follows.

01

Surface

One paint, lit from above. Every grey is worked out from a single tone, so the body, the raised parts and the knurling stay in step when it changes.

02

Legends

Type is printed on the body, small and in capitals, or it glows from behind glass. Nothing else carries text.

03

States

Every control has the same states, shown the same way. Hover lifts the legend and the light on a raised part. Focus is one ring that appears at once. Disabled dims the part and it takes no input.

04

Motion

Seven durations and five curves, kept in the site’s stylesheet and used by every part here. Duration steps up with distance. Parts go down fast and come up slower; things leave one step faster than they arrive; only parts with a physical stop overshoot. With reduced motion on, travel becomes an instant change and light still fades.

05

Space, shape and layers

Padding, gaps and corners come from the site’s scales. A cell is padded 16 by 24 with 12 between its parts; the compact density is 12 by 16 with 8.

06

Controls

Each one works: turn, slide and press them. Knobs also answer to the wheel and the arrow keys.

07

Entry

Text goes into a well cut in the body. Every input sits in a field, which gives it a legend and room for help or an error.

08

Selection

Choosing one of a few, or switching several on. A bank is the hardware radio group: press one and the others pop up.

09

Actions

The key in its ranks. A panel takes one primary key at most; quiet keys are for the least important action.

10

Navigation and overlays

Anything that opens does so from the control that opened it. Press the keys to open each one; hover or focus the last key for a tooltip.

11

Feedback

The system reports with light. A lamp carries the status, and the words say what happened and what to do. Each status colour is at least 4.5:1 against its body in both finishes.

12

Data

Content that isn’t a control: labels, counts, modules of text, and rows ruled with the same seam that divides a panel.

13

Assemblies

The parts put together. The tuner is the control panel from the Chladni cover, rebuilt from the system; the channel strip is a second arrangement to check the parts hold up elsewhere.

14

Rules

How to use the parts. These hold for every assembly.

Accessibility

  • Text on a body is at least 4.5:1 against it. Lamps, value rings and the focus ring are at least 3:1. The tokens meet this in both finishes.
  • Every control works from the keyboard. Tab reaches it; Space or Enter works keys and switches; arrows move knobs, banks, segmented controls and tabs.
  • A lamp never carries meaning alone. Pair it with words.
  • The speckle in a plain key’s paint is iron: it stands up to the pointer and is thrown back by a press. It is decoration under the legend, never the only sign of anything, and it is switched off for reduced motion. Coloured, quiet, pad and disabled keys have none; data-plain turns it off on one key.
  • A control with no visible text needs an aria-label.
  • Targets are at least 24px. The tick box is 18px, so always wrap it in its label.
  • The focus ring appears at once and is never animated.

Content

  • A legend is one or two words with no punctuation. Write it in sentence case; the stylesheet sets the capitals.
  • A key says what it does: a verb, or a verb and a noun. “Save preset”, not “OK”.
  • Units follow the value in a dim legend: Hz, dB.
  • Numbers on a display use tabular figures and a true minus sign.
  • An error says what is wrong and what to do about it.
  • Running text is sentence case in the text face. Never set a sentence in capitals.

Layout

  • A body is a row of cells divided by seams. One job per cell.
  • The legend sits above its control; a state lamp sits below or beside it.
  • One primary key and one accent-filled part per body.
  • A body on the bench casts a shadow; a body inside a layout uses .m-panel--flat.
  • Below 480px a body marked .m-panel--reflow stacks its cells. Controls keep their size.
  • The light finish is a grey body, not a white one. Metal turns silver and meters show dark segments in a pale window; display glass stays black.

Icons

  • Lucide, the set the site already uses, at 12 or 16px with a 1.5 stroke, in the legend colour.
  • An icon replaces a legend only for actions everyone knows: previous, next, search, close, play.
  • An icon-only key needs an aria-label and a tooltip.

Naming

  • The site owns the scales: --space-*, --radius-*, --text-*, --dur-*, --ease-*, and raw colours as --c-*.
  • Matte tokens are roles, named --m-*, that point at those scales. A part never uses a raw value.
  • Classes are .m-part, .m-part__piece and .m-part--variant.
  • State lives in ARIA attributes (aria-pressed, aria-checked, aria-invalid), not in classes. The one exception is .is-on for a lamp.
