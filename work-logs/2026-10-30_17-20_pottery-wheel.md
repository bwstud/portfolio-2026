# Pottery wheel: first pass, from wedged clay to a fired piece in a viewer

## Project Context
A new tinkering project, a sibling to Bonsai. You pick a clay and an amount, throw it on a spinning wheel with a set of tools, glaze it and fire it, then look at it in a cinematically lit viewer. Undo, rewind and saved versions work throughout.

## Work Performed
- **Clay model (`clay.js`)**:
  - The clay is a stack of 2 mm rings, each with an inner and outer radius, plus an angle × height map for carved marks.
  - Every hand tool conserves volume:
    - Pulling thins the wall and stretches it upward.
    - Pushing a wall out thins it.
    - Opening spreads the clay out.
  - Centring works against a 7 mm starting wobble.
  - Wetness affects grip and slump.
  - A strain measure covers thin walls and overhang, and causes sagging and collapse.
  - A spin that is too fast flares the wall outward.
  - Tested in Node: a 1.2 kg throw holds its volume after the per-stroke volume correction.
- **Tools**:
  - Hands: centre and cone, open, pull up, shape.
  - Tools: rib, sponge, needle (cuts the rim, with the offcut flying off), trimming loop.
  - Detail, on a still wheel: carve and thumb press. With the wheel spinning, these cut rings instead.
  - Add water.
- **Input**:
  - Tools read the pointer in the vertical plane facing the camera, so 3D drag, touch and the 2D cross-section card all drive the same model.
  - Guides show where a tool is touching: a section outline, a contact ring and a highlight band.
  - Pen pressure adds to the pressure setting.
  - Two fingers orbit and zoom; right-drag orbits.
- **Wheel**: inertia, a speed slider with a green band for the selected tool's best speed, auto pedal, pause on Space, direction toggle, and a pedal that tilts with speed.
- **History**: every stroke is a step, with undo/redo and a scrubber. Rewinding and then working discards the later steps, and three or more are first auto-saved as a version.
- **Saving**:
  - Named versions with section thumbnails, stored in localStorage with quantized marks.
  - The session auto-restores on reload; `?new` skips it.
- **Scenes**: four Poly Haven HDRIs projected onto the floor (workshop, pine attic, veranda, fireside). The key light is taken from each HDRI's brightest patch. Dust motes indoors, flicker by the fire.
- **Glaze stage**: flat base or trimmed foot ring, and nine glazes. A custom shader handles the glaze look:
  - the dip line runs down from the rim, with drips
  - glaze breaks thin over rims and ridges, and pools in carved lines and throwing rings
  - crackle, ash rivulets, shino carbon spots and iron speckle
  - inside: same glaze, white liner or bare
  - a dip-depth slider and a thickness slider
- **Firing and viewer**:
  - Kiln overlay with a temperature count, and firing shrinkage applied.
  - A seamless sweep with a plinth sized to the piece, and four lighting looks: gallery, window, low key, daylight.
  - Camera shots, turntable, depth of field, vignette and grain.
  - A plaque you can rename, PNG export.
- **Sound**: wheel hum that follows rpm, a clay-rub noise while touching, a thud when the clay lands, and a mute toggle.
- **Mobile**: the tool rail becomes a scrolling bottom dock, the side panel and glaze panel become bottom sheets, and the view is offset to stay clear of panels. A first-touch hint explains one finger versus two.
- Gallery card and thumbnail added to `tinkering/index.html`; HDRI credits in `pottery/assets/CREDITS.md`.
- Verified in headless Chrome (SwiftShader) at 1440, 1280, 1100, 960 and 390 px:
  - a full mouse-driven throw
  - touch strokes
  - undo/redo, versions and session restore
  - glaze, fire and every viewer look
  - a clean console apart from the site-wide favicon 404

## Key Decisions
- Hand tools act on a whole ring (the wheel is turning) and use relative drags for shape and cone, so holding still never moves clay unexpectedly. Carving and pressing on a still wheel are the only local marks.
- The cross-section card shares the same input model as the 3D view. That gives precision on desktop and a reliable fallback on phones.
- The glaze preview shows the fired result; the viewer is a separate photo set, not the studio.
