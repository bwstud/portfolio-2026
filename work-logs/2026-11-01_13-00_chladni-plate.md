# Chladni plate: a sand-covered steel plate that draws its resonances

## Project Context
A new tinkering experiment that makes sound visible. A sand-covered steel plate floats in a dark space, with a toggle switch and a frequency knob at the bottom of the screen. At each resonance, the sand gathers on the lines where the plate holds still. It's modelled on the classic Chladni plate demonstration video.

## Work Performed
- **Physics (`chladni/plate.js`, no three.js)**:
  - 34 modes of a square plate with free edges, driven at the centre, from 73 Hz to 4.8 kHz. Each mode shape is cos(nπx)cos(mπy) + cos(mπx)cos(nπy) with n and m even, since a centre drive only reaches those.
  - Each mode is detuned slightly so that modes that would share a frequency don't sound together.
  - The plate's steady response at the current frequency is computed as a complex sum over nearby modes, with each resonance at Q 45, on a 192² grid along with its gradient.
- **Sand**:
  - 48,000 grains on desktop, 26,000 on phones.
  - A grain lifts and scatters only when the vibration beneath it beats a threshold, so the sand sits still between resonances.
  - Below the threshold, grains still creep downhill. Without that, the sand stopped either side of each node line and left double lines.
  - A smoothed sand-depth map gives the piles their height and shades the metal around them.
- **Tuning**: done in Node first by rendering depth maps to images.
  - Patterns form over about 1.5 s on a resonance.
  - 1.2% off a resonance, a faint figure forms; 3% off gives a partial one; 10% off, nothing moves.
- **Scene (`chladni/app.js`)**:
  - A brushed steel plate (anisotropic physical material) lit only by a dark studio environment: an overhead softbox, a warm strip and a cool rim.
  - A centre washer, nut and bolt, and a post that fades into the dark.
  - Sand drawn as round grains shaded by the key light, with alpha-to-coverage on a 4× MSAA target.
  - Grains hop while the plate rings, and the plate slowly bobs as if floating.
  - Dust motes, a faint halo that brightens as the plate rings, light bloom, a vignette and grain.
  - The camera frame fits the free space between the header and the dock on any screen, using a view offset.
- **Controls (`chladni/index.html`)**:
  - A bat-lever toggle that flips with a foreshortening scale, with a pilot lamp that glows harder at resonance.
  - A knurled knob where one full turn is one octave. Turn it by circling it, or drag up and down over its centre.
  - The knob also takes the mouse wheel (Shift for coarse) and the keyboard: arrows for a twentieth of a semitone, Shift for a semitone, PageUp and PageDown for an octave.
  - A ring around the knob shows the position in the whole range, with a dot per resonance; the nearest dot lights up.
  - A readout shows Hz, the resonance as (n, m) or "Between resonances", and a resonance meter.
  - Previous and next resonance buttons glide to a resonance, for phones.
  - Global keys: Space, [ ], S. Plus a first-run hint, the shared notes card, and a "Scatter sand" button.
  - Accessibility: role switch and slider with aria values; respects reduced motion.
- **Sound**: Web Audio sine through a compressor, turned down at high frequencies and louder at resonance. A band-passed noise hiss follows how much sand is moving.
- **Gallery**: added a card and thumbnail to `tinkering/index.html`.
- **Verified in headless Chrome (SwiftShader)**:
  - The switch click forms the (4, 2) figure; a glide forms (4, 4).
  - A quarter turn of the knob moved 0.233 octaves.
  - The arrow and ] keys work; a tap on the switch on a 390 px touch layout works.
  - The page doesn't scroll sideways, and the gallery card renders.
  - Sim step: 2.1 ms for 48k grains on this Mac.

## Key Decisions
- Grains on the CPU rather than a GPU simulation: 48k grains cost about 2 ms per step, and the code stays simple and testable in Node.
- Sharp resonances (Q 45) make finding a figure a real search. The knob is multi-turn (one turn per octave) so each resonance is about a 10° twist. The ticks around the dial and the glide buttons keep it discoverable, especially on phones.
- The mode shapes are an idealised free-plate approximation, not a real plate's measured shapes, and the frequencies are illustrative.
