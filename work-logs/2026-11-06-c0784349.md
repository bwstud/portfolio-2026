## Work done
- **`tinkering/fold-dojo/engine.js`:** a flat-folding engine. The sheet is a list of convex facets, each with its outline in paper coordinates and a 2D affine placement. A fold step is a set of moves. Each move picks facets by side of a line, layer from the top, part of the sheet or a tag, and turns them about a chain of hinges. One hinge is a valley or mountain fold. Two nested hinges give a petal fold. Inside-reverse folds open the layers like a book and slip between the layers they land among. Also flips, turns, unfolds (the previous fold played backward, with its creases kept) and custom moves. Stacking order after each fold comes from comparing, for every overlapping pair, which way the layers were travelling as they landed: outer hinge first, then inner, then starting layer, sorted topologically. Shaping steps (wings spread) keep a 3D pose, and a presentation turn stands the model up.
- **`designs.js`:** crane (30 steps), paper airplane (11, on A4, A5 or Letter, with geometry from the sheet's proportions), samurai helmet (9), heart (13), cup (5). The crane's square base is a real rigid collapse: the eight triangles round the centre turn about their creases, rise into a four-pointed star, then flatten.
- **`paper.js`:** 18 colours and 14 patterns, all drawn in canvas: plain, washi, waves (seigaiha), hemp leaf (asanoha), blossom, dots, stripes, gingham, marbled (suminagashi), rainbow, kraft, newsprint, gold and silver foil. The other side can be white, the same or a second colour. Five weights from 50 to 160 gsm change thickness (drawn at 1.6× real), how much a flap curls as it moves, roughness and how strongly creases show. Square sizes are 7.5, 15, 24 and 35 cm. Creases are drawn onto the texture as each fold adds one.
- **`surfaces.js`:** desk (Poly Haven wood scan), concrete counter (Concrete Floor 02 scan, downloaded this session), cutting mat (drawn: cm grid, angle guides, numbers), rubber pad on the desk, glass table (transmission, steel frame, wooden floor below, shadow catcher).
- **`app.js` and `index.html`:** studio HDR lighting, a key light with shadows, and optional contact shadows (GTAO). **Watch** mode has transport, speed, step bar and keyboard shortcuts. **Fold it yourself** mode: the part that moves glows, amber dashes mark a valley fold and blue dots a mountain fold. You grab the paper and drag. The step follows the grabbed point's path on screen, and releasing past 45% finishes the fold. Grabbing a part that doesn't move explains why. Hint escalates and Show me plays the step. Undo is there too. Camera views (front, top, side, low), a zoom slider and follow-the-paper. The **viewer** offers six HDR settings, a plinth, the floor (grounded skybox) or floating, background blur and a turntable. Flat models stand upright in it. **Saved pieces** store design, paper settings and a thumbnail in localStorage, and reopen in the viewer. All labels are sentence case, with no uppercase styling.
- **Assets:** five HDRs and the wood scans copied from Pottery, Bonsai and Knot Dojo; concrete scans downloaded from Poly Haven (CC0). `assets/CREDITS.md` lists them all. About 13 MB in total.
- **Gallery:** Fold Dojo card added to `tinkering/index.html` after Bouquet. `thumb.jpg` (960×600) is the finished crane on the desk.

### Domino Run (`tinkering/domino-run/`)
- **Physics (`physics.js`):** Rapier 0.19.3 (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`, loaded from jsDelivr) at 240 steps a second. Dominoes start asleep and wake when touched. Each run is recorded as it's simulated, a few milliseconds a frame ahead of the playhead. A domino only gets keyframes while it moves, so long runs stay small. That recording is what makes seeking back and forth, 0.2× to 5× speed, and replays exact. Impacts come from jumps in Rapier's contact force for each pair. The extra impulse divided by the lighter body's mass gives the change in speed, which sets the loudness. This was needed because a domino's top hitting the floor raises no new collision event, since its base is already touching. The starting push sets a tipping spin on the start dominoes, which are the ones marked Start, or the first one placed if none is marked.
- **Room (`room.js`):** built entirely in code. Walls with real window openings, so the sun only comes in through the windows and throws the window frames' shadows on the floor. Sash windows with sheer curtains, the Poly Haven garden HDR outside, built-in cabinets with shaker doors, shelving towers dressed with books and vases, a 65-inch TV with a soundbar, a couch with cushions, a coffee table with a lower shelf, side tables with lamps (point lights), a plant and a picture. The floor is the Poly Haven Wood Floor scan. The room's own light is captured into the environment map (PMREM from the scene), so reflections and fill come from the room. Dominoes can stand on the floor, the coffee table, the side tables or the counter. Furniture blocks placement and is solid in the physics.
- **Dominoes (`dominoes.js`):** five styles, each with its own density, friction and sound: toppling (glossy plastic), classic (rounded, with pips on both faces drawn per tile), wooden (grain texture under the stain), acrylic (transmission) and steel. 18 colours plus any colour. Height from the standard 48 mm to 12 inches on a log slider, with chips for common sizes. A selection shows as an outline and a coloured pad on the surface, so it can still be seen from far away.
- **Editing (`layout.js`, `app.js`):** tools are Select, Place and Draw. Draw lays a line at the chosen spacing as you drag, turning at most about 26° per domino, and groups it. Place and Draw join on to the end of a nearby line (Shift places freely). Select covers click, Shift-click, box select, drag to move and double-click to pick up. You can also pick up and carry, turn with Q and E, nudge with the arrows, lock, group and ungroup, copy, paste and duplicate, delete, mark a start, undo and redo (200 steps), and the layout is kept as a draft between visits. Pasting carries on from the leading end, turned to follow it. It goes alongside instead if more dominoes already stand past that end, or if carrying on would run into something, and the message says which. Bending: a selected group shows a dot on each end. Dragging one lays the group along a curve from the pinned end, keeping its starting direction, and shows the spacing as you go, with a warning when they're too far apart to topple. Placements that overlap another domino or the furniture show red and are refused.
- **Elements (`elements.js`):** seven built in, all drawn in the current style, colour, size and spacing: straight line, quarter turn, U-turn, S-bend, spiral, split in two, and a growing chain with each domino a quarter taller. Any selection can be saved as an element. Placing one shows a ghost, joins on to a nearby line end, and turns with Q and E.
- **Effects (`effects.js`):** nine. Six fire when the domino is hit: sparks, light up, light trail, chime (several climb a scale), slow motion (playback slows around that domino) and spotlight (the room dims). Three fire when it lands: confetti, dust puff and fireworks. Each is drawn as a function of time since it fired, with seeded randomness, so scrubbing shows the same thing every time. Their times show as dots on the timeline, and playback carries on past the end of the physics until the last effect has finished.
- **Sound (`audio.js`):** synthesised with Web Audio, no samples. Each click is a burst of filtered noise plus decaying partials. The partials depend on the style (plastic, wood, acrylic, metal), and the surface sets the rest: floor thud, table knock, stone tick, soft couch. Pitch follows size, level follows impact speed and distance, sounds pan by screen position, and a small room echo is added. Effects have their own sounds (chime, pop, crackle, whoosh). There's a voice limit, and at high speed only the strongest impacts play.
- **Timeline:** restart, back 5 s, play and pause, forward 5 s, and speeds 0.2×, 0.5×, 1×, 2× and 5×. A scrub bar shows how much has been worked out so far. A status line shows how many are down and when slow motion is on. The camera follows the falling dominoes, with views for fit, room, above, floor level, couch and window, a zoom slider, and orbiting on right-drag. It's kept inside the room and out of the furniture.
- **Camera keys (after the user's first try):** the user asked for panning, and for ⌘ to control the camera as well as right-click. Now ⌘-drag orbits and ⌘⇧-drag pans, alongside right-drag (orbit), ⇧ right-drag (pan), middle-drag (pan) and scroll (zoom); ⌥ works like ⌘, and on Windows it's Ctrl. Because OrbitControls itself swaps orbit and pan whenever ⌘, Ctrl or Shift is held, the left button is mapped to the opposite action and the swap lands on the right one, and the keys are read in a capture listener before OrbitControls sees the press. ⌘-click no longer takes one domino out of its group; clicking a selected domino again does that now. Measured in headless Chrome: ⌘-drag turned the view 36.7° (right-drag: 37.4°), and ⌘⇧-drag moved the target 17.2 cm (⇧ right-drag: 17.5 cm).
- **Saving (`store.js` and `tools/lab-server.mjs`):** the local server gained `/__domino/` routes. Runs save as `tinkering/domino-run/saves/states/<id>.json` with a `.jpg` thumbnail, and elements as `saves/elements/<id>.json`. The routes take only same-origin writes and validate ids. On the deployed site, which has no server, saves fall back to localStorage, and the header says which mode is in use. `saves/` is withheld from Vercel in `.vercelignore`.
- **Lab:** a drawer in the page, opened from the header or from `domino-run/lab/` (also linked in the site's Controls menu on the home page and the Tinkering page). It has 70-odd settings across physics, room layout, camera, lighting, dominoes, timings and sounds, with live updates. A change rebuilds the room, the looks or the run as needed. Changed settings are marked and can be reset one at a time or by section. It also has test layouts (line, curve, growing sizes, off the coffee table, every style). Save writes the changed settings to `tinkering/domino-run/params.json`, which the page loads at start. It ships as `{}`.
- **Environment picker:** seven cards. The living room is live, with its own render as the picture. Kitchen, school gym, library, garage workshop, office lobby and museum hall are marked coming soon.
- **Gallery:** Domino Run card after Fold Dojo. `thumb.jpg` is a rainbow S-curve mid-fall in the sunlit patch.

## Challenges and how we solved them
- **Collapse ties:** in the square base, the two triangles of each pressed flap coincide exactly, so their order came out arbitrary and the wrong quadrant ended up in front. Fixed with a tie-break: the triangle nearer the front flap's corner goes in front.
- **White wings:** the first petal fold tucked the sides under the rising flap, so the finished crane had white wings on top. Reversing the side creases (the sides swing in and cover the flap) gives a coloured bird base and coloured wings.
- **Helmet horns:** the horn fold's region took in the bottom half too. Fixed with a boundary at the base of the horns.
- **Neck and tail hidden:** the first reverse-fold angle left both inside the wing outline. Re-angled to send the legs out at 134° so they show.
- **Airplane upside down:** the presentation turn had the wrong sign.
- **Camera framed far out:** it framed the sheet before its scale was applied. Fixed by updating the matrices first.
- **Testing:** each design was checked in Node first with top-down SVG drawings of every step, then in headless Chrome (Playwright, CPU rendering) with screenshots, a scripted drag and a save-and-reopen run.
- **Domino Run, falls counted wrong:** `fallenBy(Infinity)` counted dominoes that were never hit, because `Infinity <= Infinity` is true, so the page said "All 40 fell" when 24 had. The earlier headless counts were wrong for the same reason. Fixed the count and re-checked every layout.
- **Domino Run, effects cut off:** the timeline ended when the physics settled, so a firework on the last domino never burst. Playback now runs until the last effect that has fired is done.
- **Domino Run, table test:** the line on the coffee table stopped 30 cm short of the edge and never reached the floor line. It now runs to the edge, and all 40 fall, off the table and on along the floor.
- **Domino Run, selection hard to see:** the outline was under a pixel wide at normal zoom. Added the pad on the surface under each selected domino.
- **Domino Run, camera inside furniture:** following the run, the camera ended up inside a lamp shade. The camera is now pushed out of every piece of furniture, and the lamp shades are solid.
- **Domino Run, spiral looked like a ring:** it turned in too little per lap. It now closes in by 1.6 heights a lap.
- **Domino Run, phone header:** the buttons ran off the right edge at 390 px. Below 720 px the brand, redo, help and sound buttons are hidden.
- **Domino Run, testing:** the physics recorder was checked in Node with Rapier on its own first (line, curve, growing sizes, steel, off a table). In headless Chrome: every test layout and every built-in element falls completely (line 40/40, curve 36/36, growing 8/8, table 40/40, every style 120/120, elements 20 to 50 each). Draw, Place, click-select and drag-move were driven with the real mouse. Paste carries on from the end, and its run continues: 72 of 72 fell, and with every style, 144 of 144. A second paste goes alongside. A bent group still falls 36/36. Delete is refused while locked. Save to the project folder and reopen (88 back), saving an element to the project and loading it back, the lab saving `params.json` and the page reading it after a reload: all work. The page loads with no errors. Sound playback ran with the audio context running and no errors, but nobody listened to it.

## Unsolved problems
- Seen only in headless Chrome, which renders on the CPU. Real-time playback there was slow, because the frame step is capped at 50 ms. Frame rate, GTAO cost and glass transmission on a real GPU are not measured.
- Not tried by hand in a browser: drag feel, touch on a phone, and orbit and pan alongside the paper grab.
- Simplifications: layers don't bend round each other at creases; inside-reverse and petal folds move rigidly, so hidden layers can briefly separate mid-move; the cup's front flap folds over the front instead of tucking into the pocket; the crane's body isn't puffed out.
- Two viewer settings looked plain in testing (Japanese house behind the plinth, the floor option). Worth judging on a real screen.
- The lab server started for testing (PID 75802, port 8090) is still running and stops when the session ends.
- **Domino Run:** not tried by hand or on a real GPU. Unknowns: drag feel, trackpad orbiting, touch, frame rate with hundreds of dominoes and 4096 px shadows, and how the acrylic transmission costs. The synthesised sounds haven't been listened to.
- **Domino Run simplifications:** dominoes only stand upright on flat surfaces, turning about the vertical. A bend is a single curve from the pinned end. Groups can't be nested. Only one environment exists.
- **Domino Run, headless speed:** in headless Chrome, frames take long enough that the run is worked out slowly at 7 ms a frame. On a normal GPU that budget is 7 ms of a 16 ms frame, which should be plenty, but it hasn't been measured.
