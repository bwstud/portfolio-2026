### Kaleidoscopes (`tinkering/kaleidoscopes/`)
- **Renderer (`scope.js`):** one WebGL 2 fragment shader. Two-mirror wedges fold each pixel by angle (180°/n). Three- and four-mirror cells (equilateral, 90-45-45, 90-60-30 and square) reflect the pixel across whichever mirror it's beyond until it lands inside the cell. The number of reflections darkens it ("light lost per reflection"), and the distance to the nearest mirror draws seams. Also: lens bulge, twist, colour fringing (three folds at slightly different scales), round or full-screen eyepiece, vignette, glass sheen, tube colour, and brightness, contrast, saturation, hue and tint. The picture is a mirrored-repeat texture with mipmaps, and videos are re-uploaded every frame. `pose()` works out the cell's turn, drift, view spin and breathing at time t, and the lab's overlay uses the same function.
- **Configurations (`configs.js`):** 28 settings in six sections (mirrors, object cell, motion, glass and mirrors, eyepiece, colour), defined once. The lab builds its controls from that list. Ten built-ins: classic, snowflake, rose window, mandala, tile floor, star chart, mosaic, whirlpool, prism and antique brass. `normalise()` clamps and fills saved settings, so old saves survive new settings. Saves go to `configs.json` through the local server (it ships, so everyone sees them), or to localStorage on the deployed site. A kaleidoscope can also travel in a link as `#c=<base64url JSON>`.
- **Main page (`index.html`, `app.js`):** full-window view with two strips: kaleidoscopes, as live-rendered round thumbnails of the current picture, and pictures, the 10 gallery images plus "Your photo or video" (file picker or drop anywhere). Drag slides the picture under the mirrors, shift-drag or a two-finger twist turns it, and the wheel or a pinch magnifies (`shared.js`). The other controls: Turning on and off, reset view, snapshot (PNG), record a clip (MediaRecorder, WebM or MP4, with the video's sound if it's on), full screen, hide controls, video play/pause and sound. Keys: ← → for kaleidoscopes, [ ] for pictures, Space, S, R, F and H. The current picture's credit and licence link sits in the dock. The eyepiece is centred in the space between the header and the dock (`scope.frame`).
- **Lab (`lab/index.html`, `lab/lab.js`):** its own page, linked from the main page header, the Tinkering page's Controls menu and the home page's Controls menu (Kaleidoscope Lab).
  - **Left:** configurations grouped as built in, in the project and in this browser, each with a thumbnail and a dot when there are unsaved changes. New and Duplicate.
  - **Centre:** live preview with gestures, motion on and off, restart, and a "What the mirrors see" inset that dims the picture outside the object cell and outlines the cell as it moves. Below it, a test-picture row of the gallery plus your own file.
  - **Right:** name, description, and every setting with its value and help. A changed setting gets a dot and an Undo, and each section has its own Undo. Settings that don't apply are hidden, such as the mirror angle for triangles.
  - **Save bar:** Save (own configurations only), Save as new (the only option for built-ins), Undo changes and Delete. The header has Open in Kaleidoscopes (a share link while there are unsaved changes), Copy link, Export and Import (JSON). It warns before discarding changes or leaving the page.
- **Server (`tools/lab-server.mjs`):** `/__kaleido/ping` and `POST /__kaleido/configs` validate the list (ids, names, values that are only numbers, colours or short names, same-origin, JSON) and write `configs.json` atomically. The startup banner lists Kaleidoscopes.
- **Gallery (`gallery/`):** Hokusai's Great Wave, Van Gogh's Starry Night, Hubble's Pillars of Creation (2014), Haeckel's jellyfish and hummingbirds, Klimt's Kiss, Monet's Water Lilies (1906), all public domain. Also a peacock photo (Jatin Sindhu, CC BY-SA 4.0), red tulip fields (acediscovery, CC BY 4.0) and a Kashmir shawl motif (The Met, CC0). Licences were checked through the Commons API and the Met's object records. They're re-encoded with Pillow at quality 84 (9 MB in total), with 240 px thumbnails, and credited in `gallery/CREDITS.md`.

### Raptor Run (`tinkering/raptor-run/`)
- **Layers:** three stacked canvases. `#ground` is painted once per load or resize from a seeded random generator: grass blades batched by colour, soft tonal blotches, dirt patches, leaves, pebbles, rocks and ferns. `#stage` draws the actors each frame. `#canopy` holds trees overhanging the edges, with a blurred offset shadow, so runners pass under them.
- **Paths:** each chase picks a random edge and entry point, aims across the screen within ±0.45 rad of a point near the centre, and adds one or two sideways waypoints. A centripetal Catmull-Rom curve is sampled every 4 px into an arc-length polyline, and extends in a straight line before its start and after its end.
- **Timing:** person speed 330 px/s × build × jitter. The raptor runs 13–22% faster on the same path, and its start is set so its snout reaches the person 0.35–0.7 s after the person leaves the screen (grown by 70 px), with a minimum gap of 120 px. Once the raptor's tail is off screen, the next chase waits 1.2–4.5 s. Only one chase exists at a time.
- **Raptor:** 51 spine nodes, 6 px apart, sampled back along the person's path from the snout, with tail swing and a little torso sway. The body is a smoothed outline from a half-width profile: wedge head, thin neck, torso widest at the hips, long tail. Hind legs use a gait cycle with a duty factor of 0.38, so each foot is planted and slides back at body speed, then swings forward lifted, with an airborne moment in each stride. Haunches sit under the flanks, and the shins and three-toed feet with sickle claws land outside the body. Feathered forearms, a tail fan on three of the four colourings, yellow eyes and a dark mask, and the jaw opens slightly when close. Footfalls leave prints that fade over 9 s and kick up dust.
- **People:** 12 roles (park ranger, scientist in a lab coat, tourist, jogger, construction worker, suit with briefcase, summer dress, hiker, security guard, chef, yellow raincoat, hoodie), 4 builds (slim, average, broad, round), 7 skin tones, 9 hair colours and 7 hair styles, with hats and hoods where the role has them. Legs and arms swing in opposition, arms flail, coats and ponytails flap, and they glance back when the raptor is within 520 px.
- **Shadows:** every actor is drawn again in solid black onto an offscreen layer, which is composited once at 30% opacity and offset down and right, so overlapping parts don't double up. The offset grows a little in the airborne part of each stride.
- **Page:** Pause, Next chase, Slow motion (0.3×), Full screen and Hide (Space, N, M, F, H). A status line names who's running and counts the chases. Below 760 px everything shrinks with the screen (0.55× at 375 px wide). With reduced motion set, it starts paused and says why. A notes card, and a Tinkering card after Kaleidoscopes, with a 960×600 thumbnail captured mid-chase.

### Oscilloscope (`tinkering/oscilloscope/`)
- **Signals (`engine.js`):** two generators with ten waveforms (sine, square, triangle, saw, pulse, noise, AM, FM, heartbeat, ringing), plus frequency, amplitude, offset, phase, added noise and per-waveform extras. Every signal is a pure function of time. A `Recorder` keeps the generator settings over the last 300 s, with phase kept continuous across changes, so any moment can be redrawn exactly.
- **Sweep engine:** armed and sweeping states, trigger search with hysteresis and bisection, auto, normal and free-run modes, and a trigger position on screen. Fast timebases draw a few representative sweeps per frame, weighted to match the real sweep rate. Sweep state is checkpointed every frame so rewinding can restore it.
- **Renderer (`crt.js`):** WebGL 2 with a half-float buffer that fades like phosphor. Each trace segment is a quad lit by the exact integral of a Gaussian spot along it, divided by its length, so the beam is dim where it moves fast. Bloom, tone mapping, barrel curvature, a procedural graticule (lines or dots), glass glare and vignette.
- **Page (`index.html`, `app.js`):** Inputs, Scope and Display tabs. Y–T or X–Y; per-channel show, volts per division, position and coupling; a math trace (1 + 2, 1 − 2, 1 × 2); timebase and trigger. Six phosphors, per-channel colour, a custom colour, intensity, focus, persistence, glow, lines or dots, graticule style and brightness, curvature and glass. 12 presets. Transport: rewind at 7 speeds, pause, frame steps, a scrub bar over the recording and Live. Changing a generator while rewound clears the later recording and says so. Keys Space, R, L, F, H, S and ← →.

### Cymascope (`tinkering/cymascope/`)
- **Physics (`physics.js`):** a round dish whose surface can only hold Neumann Bessel modes J_m(k r) cos(m θ) with J'_m(k R) = 0. Each tone gets its wavenumber from the capillary-gravity dispersion relation (with depth), and every mode near it answers with a resonance curve whose width comes from viscosity (2νk²) plus a wall term. Five liquids. Bessel functions come from rational approximations and Miller's recurrence. Zeros of J'_m are found by scanning and bisection, cached in bins. Radial profiles (J and its slope, 512 samples out to 1.5 R) are cached per mode.
- **Symmetry lock:** mixing unrelated modes gave mazes instead of mandalas, so modes whose fold count isn't a multiple of the strongest mode's (m ≥ 3) are suppressed by (1 − lock)². There's also a fixed fold count option.
- **Renderer (`render.js`):** one WebGL 2 pass rebuilds height, slope and curvature (Laplacian = −k²h for each mode) from up to 16 modes stored in an RG16F profile texture. Six lighting models: ring of lights (continuous or discrete LEDs, reflecting off the slope, plus light from the camera), shadowgraph (1/|1 + d·∇²h|), raking light, height map, contours and schlieren. Colour split runs the light three times at different strengths for rainbow fringes. Then a palette, colour by brightness, slope direction or height, mirror folds, zoom and rotation, the dish rim, bloom, grain, vignette and invert.
- **Page (`index.html`, `app.js`):** Sound, Dish, Light and Style tabs. Two tones with waveform partials, a sweep, a microphone source (Web Audio analyser, the two loudest peaks), and audio out through oscillators. Modes ease in and out over about 0.18 s. A strobe-drift slider sets how fast the surface seems to breathe. Pause, reverse and step, drag to turn, scroll to zoom, double-click to reset, 12 presets and a readout (frequency and note, wavelength, dominant mode and order, symmetry, resonance). Keys Space, R, T, F, H, S and ← →.
- **Tinkering cards:** Oscilloscope after Raptor Run, and Cymascope after Oscilloscope, each with a 960×600 thumbnail.

### Spectrogram (`tinkering/spectrogram/`)
- **Sources (`signals.js`):** 11 built-in sources at 48 kHz, mostly additive (Chebyshev recurrence or rotating oscillators): chirp (log or linear glide, harmonics), piano (stiff-string partials with per-partial decay; arpeggio, chords or melody), voice (harmonics weighted by interpolated formants for five vowels, vibrato, breath, optional pauses), birdsong (up to three birds, six note shapes), whale song, FM (drone with index swell, or bells), phone (dial tone, DTMF digits, ringing), hidden message (text rasterised to 44 rows of tones, so it reads on the spectrogram), drum machine (three beats, swing), noise (white, pink or brown, with a resonant sweep) and an audio file. Switching sources crossfades over 25 ms.
- **Analysis (`analysis.js`):** an 87 s sample history (2^22), a radix-2 FFT, seven windows (rectangle, Hann, Hamming, Blackman–Harris, Gaussian, Kaiser, flat top) normalised so a full-scale sine reads 0 dB, zero padding, and a peak refined by a parabola. A column cache keyed by absolute column number fills newest first within 7 ms a frame, so changing any setting re-analyses the whole history.
- **Renderer (`render.js`):** columns live in an R16F texture used as a ring (up to 4096 columns × 4096 bins), plus an R32I texture saying which column each row holds, so stale rows show as silence. One pass maps every pixel through the scale (linear, log, mel) and layout (scrolling, waterfall, polar) and takes the loudest of up to 8 bins per pixel. Ridgelines are one instanced draw, each line a fill then a stroke, so nearer lines hide the ones behind. Then bloom, scanlines, grain, vignette and invert.
- **Page (`index.html`, `app.js`, `capture.js`):** Sound, Analysis, View and Style tabs. The microphone uses an AudioWorklet and is resampled to 48 kHz. Files are decoded with an OfflineAudioContext. Speakers play the generated history as scheduled buffers, and the display clock follows the audio clock while they're on. A window plot shows shape and leakage. 2D overlay: axes, piano keys, live spectrum, loudest-pitch trace, harmonic cursor and hover readout. Pause, drag, step or scrub to look back, Live to return. 14 presets, which clear the history. Keys Space, L, T, F, H, S and ← →.
- **Tinkering card:** after Cymascope, with a 960×600 thumbnail of the Vowels preset.

## Challenges and how we solved them
- **Invoice in a shipped folder:** `fonts/` is on the Vercel allowlist, so committing or deploying the order folder would have published the invoice. The loose font files are byte-identical to the order's `WOFF2/` copies (checked with shasum), so only those were committed and the order folder is ignored by both git and Vercel.

- **Wikimedia rate limits:** the Commons search API returned HTTP 429 after a few queries, and thumbnails must use standard widths (1920 worked, 2000 was refused). The four blocked downloads were retried in the background 30 s apart. Searching stopped there, so the tenth image came from the Met's open-access API instead. The Met has retired its v1 search, so v1.1 was used, and objects were filtered by `isPublicDomain` one by one.
- **Licences:** NASA's Juno colour images are often CC BY-NC-SA (citizen-processed), so they weren't used. The Landsat Bahamas image was only 750 px wide and the ISS aurora frame was mostly grey cloud, so the shawl was chosen instead.
- **JPEG size:** sips ignored `formatOptions` and wrote 2 MB files. Pillow had to run from a scratch folder without `-I` to find its site-packages.
- **Eyepiece behind the dock:** the first screenshots showed the round view centred on the window, with its bottom third under the controls. The shader now takes a centre and size (`uCenter`, `uM`), and the main page computes the frame between the header and dock each frame (full window when controls are hidden).
- **Phone header:** at 375 px the last header button was clipped. The back link shrinks to an arrow and the buttons tighten, so the header ends at 367 px.
- **Testing (headless Chrome via Playwright, real lab server on port 8790):** all ten kaleidoscopes render as live thumbnails, and seven were screenshotted full size. Picture switching, drag and wheel, and the credit line were checked. In the lab: a built-in was edited, Save as new wrote `configs.json` through the server, an in-place save changed it again, the main page opened it with `?config=`, and the overlay showed. A generated WebM through the file picker played through the mirrors with changing frames. The phone layout doesn't scroll sideways. The only console error was the site-wide missing `/favicon.ico`. The test configuration was removed afterwards, so `configs.json` ships as `[]`.
- **Another session sent replacement code:** while this was being built, another Claude session sent its own `lab/index.html`, `lab.js` and gallery card. They weren't used. Its HTML used IDs its own script didn't, its font path was one level short, its save would have written the built-ins into the project file, and its card didn't match the gallery's markup.
- **Koi reference:** marinabudarina.github.io/koi is a 630 KB minified bundle (canvas 2D), so only the overhead idea was used and everything was written from scratch.
- **Raptor too far back:** the first speed ratio (1.32–1.48×) put the raptor 400–500 px behind as they entered, so early screenshots showed only the person. Dropping it to 1.13–1.22× gives about one body length behind at entry, closing to almost nothing at the exit.
- **Raptor read as a crocodile:** zoomed 2× screenshots showed a long, flat, lizard-like body with chevron "scutes" and hidden legs. It took three passes: a theropod profile (big head, thin neck, hips widest), haunches under the flanks instead of plates on the back, broken side stripes instead of chevrons, no neck crest, and feet set 13 px outside the hips with longer toes.
- **People spread-eagled:** stride reach was 1.05 × shoulder width, so legs stuck far out. It's now 0.62×, with thinner limbs and a quicker cadence. People were also made 15% larger, with outlines, so they read against the grass.
- **Rebase:** the other machine pushed PR #12 (palette, Söhne type, coloured sections, Work dropdown removed) while Kaleidoscopes was still a local commit. Both local commits rebased onto it cleanly. Afterwards both Controls menus still had Kaleidoscope Lab, the Tinkering page had both new cards, and the home page, Tinkering, Kaleidoscopes and Raptor Run all served 200 locally.
- **Testing (headless Chrome via Playwright, local server on 8080):** about ten chases at 1440×900, with 2× zoomed close-ups. Also checked at 375×812 with touch emulation: no sideways scroll, header ends at 359 px. No script errors. The only console error was the site-wide missing favicon.
