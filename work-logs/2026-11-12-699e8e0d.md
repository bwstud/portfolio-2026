## Work done

**Chladni cover** (`lab/hook/chladni.html`, shipped as `index.html`)
- A flat 2D rewrite of the plate, not the three.js lab project. Up to about 120,000 grains simulated on the CPU and drawn as WebGL points. The field is a blend of two Chladni figures, cos(nπu)cos(mπv) ± cos(mπu)cos(nπv), sampled on a 4px grid; grains slide down the slope of its square and hop where the plate moves. A "rest" band near the nodal lines gives the sand width instead of hairlines.
- The plate is as wide as the page's longer side, so the page shows the middle band and every figure is symmetric about the centre.
- One frequency value drives everything. Ten figures sit on a pentatonic scale from 220 to 740 Hz (chosen for a pleasant tone; not physical resonances). Left alone, the frequency drifts to a neighbouring note every 4.8s with a doubled smootherstep, so it crawls as a figure forms and runs in between. It opens on a random figure.
- Cursor parts the grains (130px). Click sends a ring (strength 2.9). Displaced grains live in a separate "offset" channel with a spring, so they heal to where they lay.
- Sentence arrives word by word, centred; "I'm Brian." 42px in Ochre, sentence 68px. The sand fades only behind the sentence.
- Controls panel on dark glass: slide switch for sound (off by default; Space toggles), tuning knob ported from the lab (one turn per octave), Hz readout animated with NumberFlow 0.6.2 from jsDelivr, previous/next jump buttons (`[` and `]`). Touching the knob stops the drift; it resumes 3.5s later.
- Hovering or keyboard-focusing Recent Work pulls every grain into the button; on leave they spring back (faster spring for 1.6s).
- `?ff=<seconds>` fast-forwards the simulation for screenshots (`&pulse=x,y,age`, `&mouse=x,y`, `&gather=±s`).

**Cover switcher** (`lab/hook/covers.js`): bottom-right "Covers" menu listing the prototypes; number keys jump to one. Add a cover by adding a page and one line to `COVERS`.

## Challenges and how we solved them

- **Blank canvas on first run.** `requestAnimationFrame` can report a time slightly before `performance.now()` at start, which made the figure index −1. Clamped elapsed time at zero.
- **No way to drive a browser.** The Chrome extension wasn't connected, so everything was checked with headless Chrome screenshots. Virtual time barely advances a heavy animation loop, hence the `?ff` fast-forward. Headless also resizes the window once after load (813 → 900 high), which re-seeded the grains; resize now rescales positions and the fast-forward re-runs at the final size.
- **Sand collapsed to hairlines and sparse figures.** Added the rest band and base jitter, and switched to higher modes.
- **Pulse cleared the whole screen.** Grains were surfing the ring. Lowered strength and moved pushes to the healing offset channel.
- **Hover fill came out as a fuzzy blob.** Gathered grains were springing toward a target while the plate kept moving them underneath. They now track their on-screen position directly.
- **Cymascope cover "not working" (timings, Fill screen, then an empty ring).** The plain `python3 -m http.server` let the browser cache files, so the user ran an old `app.js` without the `apply` hook; the cover waited for it forever, so pulse and saves did nothing. While in that state the first version auto-saved `drive: 0`, which later loaded as a flat, empty dish. Fixes: a no-cache server, versioned script addresses, a fallback when `apply` is missing, and dropping a zero drive from saved looks on load. This diagnosis is inferred from the user's screenshot and reports; their browser state was never inspected.
- **Opacity would have faded the control panel too** if applied to the iframe, so it is applied to the canvas inside it.

## Lessons learned

- Serve prototypes with `Cache-Control: no-store`. Stale files cost three rounds of confusion.
- The user iterates in small visual steps and judges by eye and ear; give the address, say what was and wasn't verified, and keep moving.
- The user prefers the Cymascope's own controls over duplicates on the cover (removed "Fill screen" in favour of Style → Fill the frame; moved Distort into a tab).
- `/pick-ui-library` picks from Emil Kowalski's list: NumberFlow fit the Hz readout; the switch redesign wasn't covered and was done in CSS.
