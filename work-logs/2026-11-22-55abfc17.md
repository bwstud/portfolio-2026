## Goals

The user first asked whether I could use the `find-skills` skill when told to. I said yes: I would load it first, search for fitting skills, check before installing anything, and prefer skills they already have (`design-taste`, `impeccable`, `animate`, the GSAP set).

The main request was a new experiment: an interactive game/lesson like Music of the Spheres (MOTS), teaching the Kardashev scale. In the user's words, it should be "fun and interactive, but educate me as to what the different levels of the scale really mean", using comparisons a person can relate to. The Eames Office's *Powers of Ten* is the named influence.

The process the user asked for:
1. Study MOTS and understand what succeeded there.
2. Write a proposal.
3. Have a broad council (art directors, educators, game designers, science communicators, animators, astrophysicists, futurists, design engineers and others) review, critique and revise it, drawing on prior art.
4. Plan and build v1.
5. Have the council score the build 1–10 on 17 dimensions: clarity, persuasiveness, creativity, beauty, design craft, engineering craft, delight, usability, thoroughness, surprise, elegance, performance, innovation, completeness, thoughtfulness, tastefulness, satisfaction.
6. Revise and re-score until every dimension is a 10.

Standing rules that shaped the work: the `design-taste` skill (loaded at the start), the Matte system, typed number fields on every numeric control, no extra labels or eyebrows, no all-caps headings, the work-log rule, and "never test-write `*-versions.js`".

**Earth data.** The NASA texture download was blocked, so `earth.png` (1440×720 greyscale, 62 KB) was baked by scratchpad `bake/bake-earth.cjs` from Natural Earth land, GeoNames towns, ISO country codes and OWID per-capita energy, fetched via jsDelivr and OWID. 0 = sea, 1 = land, 2–255 = night light, weighted by each country's per-capita energy.

**The piece:** `experiments/kardashev/`, "Powers of Watts".
- `zoom.js`: one log-scale zoom (z = log10 of frame width in metres) from a person by a fire to the Milky Way. It exports `STOPS`, `powerAt`, `zAt` and `createZoom`. Smoothstep node hand-offs (earth → sun → galaxy) avoid precision loss.
- `field.js`: a world-locked halftone. Its pitch halves at every octave of zoom, so new dots grow between the old and there are never seams.
- `plates.js`: the fields. The ground runs from fire to yard, town, region and globe (the globe comes from `earth.png`), then the Sun and the galaxy with dust lanes. People, riders, logs and items are part of the field too.
- `rail.js`: prints 10⁰–10³⁷ W with the Kardashev bands, the type years, marks for a body, a city and humanity, and the chapter notches. It takes drag and arrow keys.
- `audio.js`: from MOTS, with a pedal/settlement whir voice added. `data.js` holds the constants and the 14-country table.
- `app.js`: the shell. It handles the router, card, goals with chord and pulse, finale, done-set (`kardashev:done`), coda, overture, dials, phone sheet and the `window.__kardashev` hook.
- The Matte chrome, typed number fields on every fader and the phone sheet follow the MOTS patterns.

**Six chapters** in `chapters/`:

| Chapter | Title | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| `body` | "You run at a hundred watts" | Each press is a stroke, and 2 a second gives 75 W. Your breath runs out. Riders join until the kettle boils. |
| `share` | "Two dozen bodies work for you, all day" | Pick a country. Bodies stand in the yard as copper dots, in blocks of ten. |
| `today` | "Seven-tenths of the way is one five-hundredth" | Growth rate and year faders, with a K-over-time plot. |
| `type1` | "Type I: where does the heat go?" | Two ways to get there: sunlight panels, or power made on Earth, with its warming. |
| `type2` | "Type II: a star that goes dark" | A Dyson swarm at 1 AU, a visible/infrared switch and a spectrum. |
| `type3` | "Type III: the galaxy is too wide to hurry" | A settlement front, with speed and stop-length faders. |

## Challenges and how we solved them

- **No NASA texture from the shell.** Baked the Earth from Natural Earth, GeoNames towns and OWID energy data, all via jsDelivr and OWID.
- **The first plates fell apart in the middle of the zoom.** Per-plate dot sets turned into huge squares and the galaxy was faint. The fix was one screen-space halftone with octave-nested dots (`field.js`, rewritten `plates.js`).
- **The halftone painted under the card and panel**, and gave up on wide frames. It now clips to the open stage; the cell cap went from 60k to 250k. `maxPx: Infinity` would have produced NaN alpha, so `zoom.js` was patched.
- **Invented numbers.** Interpolating between stops printed powers like 3.7×10²¹ W for the Earth–Moon frame. Now a dash shows between stops (`chapLive` plus a home-z check).
- **Rail label clashes.** "a body" overlapped "your share" (mark removed), the Type labels were clipped (right-aligned near the edge), and later "Type I/II" printed over each other on a phone (narrow branch uses I/II/III with the collision pass).
- **Rail variable clash** (`nx` declared twice) stopped the page loading. Renamed it. Headless console capture (`logs.mjs`) found it.
- **Convoluted `powerHTML`.** Replaced by `supHTML`, moved into `data.js` to avoid importing from `app.js`.
- **Coda didn't fire in a test.** Cause was test setup (done-set and reload), not the app; verified with a real reload from a fresh done-set.
- **Galaxy arms read as rings.** Jitter fixed the moiré but not the shape; the star model got a wider pitch and a wandering phase. The per-frame cost this added was removed by caching.

## Lessons learned

- The council's best fixes were structural (one drawing grammar, honest dashes between stops, an on-stage reward per chapter), not cosmetic. Sending each round back to the same council agent kept scores calibrated.
- A halftone whose pitch halves per octave of zoom gives a seamless zoom; per-plate dot sets don't.
- Test scripts and screenshots can lie: `shots5/06-type3-done` was captured before the galaxy fade ran, so the reward looked missing when it worked.
- Hard-coded values drift when a constant changes (the rail years against `TODAY_YEAR`); compute them.
- Council reports arrived as subagent hand-backs, not user messages; treated as reports, not instructions.
