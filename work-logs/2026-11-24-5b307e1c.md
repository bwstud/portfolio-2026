**Servers:** Groom Lake was already being served on 8081. Mazlan's local server kept dropping requests, so a threaded no-cache server was started for it on 8084.

**First version:**

- **Checked** headless in real Chrome: 1–3 Groom Lake tiles and usually 1–2 Mazlan tiles played at once. Pause, off-screen and reduced motion behaved.

**Second version (shipped), after "all the screens need to be moving" and longer loops:**

  - In the clear middle of each wall no screen repeats. Repeats show only at the faded edges, because each column cycles 3–4 clips.

**Weight, after "reduce the weight while keeping the effect":**
- Measured five encodings on four kinds of clip with SSIM against a near-lossless reference, then compared zoomed text frames.
- Now 24fps (was 30), H.264 `-crf 30 -preset veryslow -tune animation`. The total went from 6.1MB to 3.9MB.
- Each clip also has an AV1 copy (`NN.av1.mp4`, SVT-AV1 `-crf 60 -preset 4 tune=0`): 2.5MB in total, the same SSIM as the H.264 and indistinguishable when zoomed.
- `drift-wall.js` uses AV1 only when `navigator.mediaCapabilities.decodingInfo` reports supported, smooth and power-efficient (hardware) decoding, because 17 software AV1 decodes would cost battery. Otherwise it uses H.264.
- Posters are 0.8MB. A visitor fetches only the clips in view: about 1.3MB per wall with H.264, or 0.9MB with AV1.
- Smaller frames (576 wide) would save about another 20% but soften text. Offered, not done.
- `record.mjs` now writes both encodes, and `ENCODE_ONLY=1` re-encodes from the raw recordings without re-recording.

**Verified:** in real Chrome (headless, `channel:'chrome'`), the H.264 path and a forced AV1 path each played all 17 visible Groom Lake tiles with no errors. Headless Chrome on this M2 Pro reports AV1 as not power-efficient, so it uses H.264. After merging, the live site served the clips (`video/mp4`, `image/webp`) and played 17 Groom Lake and 12 Mazlan tiles with no errors.

## Challenges and how we solved them

- **Cards blurred until mid-window:** the scroll-linked ranges ended at `cover 40–50%`. Replaced with a trigger-then-play entrance.
- **Scroll push froze the Mazlan wall:** running a CSS animation backwards from `currentTime` 0 finishes it, and the browser drops it. Fixed by moving the clock many loops forward; the whole push was then reverted.
- **Names arriving far too late** (screen recording): the name plate was driven by the board's lamp (a third down), not the reveal (an eighth up). Fixed with one sequence and `recordreveal`.
- **Titles and tags out of order:** the ink's invisible opening delayed titles, and tags sat visible ahead of everything. Fixed by skipping the first 20% of the ink and holding tags until the title is legible.
- **A link to a later record left an earlier one blank:** an instant jump crosses from below to above without an IntersectionObserver callback. Fixed with `settle()`.
- **Menu misaligned:** each row reserved an invisible lamp lane before its text. Fixed with a left lamp lane and a padded head.
- **Cover texture hidden:** the WebGL plate and field shaders painted opaque Night. Made them transparent.
- **Glint too fast, then invisible in captures:** the first version crossed the letters in under 100ms. After slowing it, timed screenshots missed it, so progress was logged per frame in the page, the path tightened, and screenshots taken at paused points.
- **Tests ignored scrolling:** under Lenis, `window.scrollTo` may not take effect. Use `siteLenis.scrollTo(y, {immediate: true})`.
- **Metal gradient dull:** the light bands didn't line up with the capitals. Realigning them didn't save the look, and the user rejected it.
- **"Lost" text sequence:** not reproduced. See Unsolved problems.
- **Playwright's bundled Chromium can't play H.264:** wall playback was tested in real Chrome.
- **This ffmpeg has no WebP encoder:** posters are made through a Chrome canvas.
- **Mazlan Home clip caught by the "What's your role?" welcome:** setup now dismisses it before filming.
- **Apps and spec-preview clips missed their controls:** found the right selectors and re-recorded.
- **Engineer health clip didn't return to its first frame:** given a dissolve at the loop point and re-recorded.
- **Scrolling in the wall recorder ran far too slowly** (clips came out 15–18s): `scroll` was made to keep to its time. Affected clips were re-recorded, and the Reports and Builder welcome clips were fixed and re-recorded.
- **Prototypes had moved on since the wall stills:** the Groom Lake audit checklist page no longer exists (the record's side panels were filmed instead). The History tab was unreadable at tile size in the first version (replaced with the theme switch).
- **Hero recorder too slow:** a 2x PNG per frame took most of a second and the steps couldn't be seen. Added a low-resolution draft mode.
- **Hero recorder hung after "Apply to filters":** the screenshot stalled and the clock stopped advancing. No dialogs or view transitions were found. Stall timeouts and clock nudges were tried, then the recorder was rewritten on Playwright's clock; the filter draft then ran in 16s with every step firing. The root cause of the stall wasn't established.
- **First intake master was short** (13.5s against 22.7s for the draft): Claude suspected Send cut the dictation off. Waiting for the full dictation and re-recording fixed it.
- **A blocked `rm -f`** on a scratchpad frame folder was not retried; a new folder was used.
- **Video layers never rendered a frame:** a seek that lands on the frame already shown fires `seeked` but presents no new frame, so `requestVideoFrameCallback` never fired and the render waited forever. The wait now also resolves after 120ms.
- **Shots mistimed or misframed in the first look:** the filter card's shot opened on the "Reading your description" spinner (start moved from 2.95 to 3.12s), the build request was framed too wide (now the composer only), and the landed shot showed a sliver of the nav rail (cropped from x=72).
- **Central log location:** this project's central logs sit directly in `~/.claude/work-logs/` and also in `~/.claude/work-logs/Portfolio 2026/`. Copies have been kept in both.

## Lessons learned

- The user's names: "project name" is the small mono name (MAZLAN BUILDER), "tagline" is the large title, "attr tags" is the mono tag list, and "info cards" are the fields.
- "Intro montage" / "hero montage" for Groom Lake means the film at the top of the project panel (sheet), not the drift wall.
- The user doesn't want scroll speed or the pointer moving ambient pieces. Entrances should be tight and in reading order.
- When two scripts animate one block, give them one clock. Separate triggers drift apart.
- Menu rules: the header aligns with the items under it and is proportional to the rows; rows have seams and lamps; menus never scroll; rows get no magnetic surface.
- Shading under a docked element should have no hard or solid edge.
- For the board: things leaving should fade before the frame shrinks, hover shouldn't light anything the pointer isn't on, and highlights should fade rather than snap. Hover hit areas must span a row's gaps or they blink.
- A painted-on metal text gradient was rejected; a one-time glint was what the user chose to try. No verdict on the glint yet.
- Changing Matte changes every menu that uses it. Say so when shipping.
- Drift-wall clips have no cursor. A mix of moving and still screens looked odd to the user, even though Claude had argued it would look natural; every screen should move. Loops should lean toward 12s.
- Both prototypes are fully coded, in `~/Projects/Personal/`, so clips can be re-recorded by script. Prototype files are the user's: drive them, don't edit them.
- For interface video, AV1 at the same SSIM was about 40–65% of the H.264 size here, but use it only where decoding is in hardware when many play at once.
- Builder wall clips took about a minute each to record because setup plays the story to the right moment. Groom Lake clips took about 40s each.
- When shipping from a branch made from main, say which other uncommitted changes are **not** going out. The user assumed the problem-statement rewrite was included.
